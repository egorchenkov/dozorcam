Your cameras, your Telegram, no cloud

Dozorcam watches IP cameras on your own Linux box, recognises people — not leaves, shadows or the cat — and sends a snapshot with the person boxed and a clip of the moment to Telegram: right into your chat, to a family group, or to a topic per camera. The whole system is run from the chat.

Who it is for

For people who already have IP cameras and want to know when someone actually walks in — without a vendor cloud, a monthly fee or yet another app.

🏡

House or summer cottage

A few cameras at the gate, the door and the yard. You get a message when a person shows up — and not every time the wind moves a branch.

🛠

Workshop, garage, small shop

The family or the team share one Telegram group, or each person gets events in a private chat. Anyone allowed can ask a camera for a fresh frame or a clip.

🖥

Home server enthusiasts

One command on a mini PC, a home server or an ARM board. Your video never leaves your network and your chat.

What it does

Everything a small camera setup needs, and nothing that sends your yard to someone else's server.

👤

Detects people, not motion

A cheap motion gate with a per-camera noise floor, then a neural person detector (YOLOX-Tiny by default, YOLOv5/YOLOv8 optional). The threshold is calibrated per camera against the scene's own noise.

🤝

Confirmed by the camera

Cameras with their own person detection (Hikvision over ONVIF) confirm a person: when the camera says “someone is here”, Dozorcam looks closer and does not miss a small or half-hidden figure.

🪣

Ignores things that never move

A bag, a bucket or a bush that the model mistakes for a person is checked against the same spot seconds earlier — if nothing moved, it is not an event.

📸

Snapshots and clips in Telegram

A snapshot with the person boxed, a clip around that moment on a tap. Events of one camera within a minute become one post; hashtags filter the feed by camera or place.

🔀

Your delivery mode

Here in the private chat, one group without topics, a topic per camera or a topic per location. Switch any time with /mode — nothing is lost.

📍

Camera map and cards

One pinned map with every camera, its state and today's events. Tap a camera for its card: frame, clip, pause, notifications, name, location, settings.

🔎

Add cameras in a minute

/add finds cameras (ONVIF, WS-Discovery), or tries the typical RTSP paths of Reolink, Tapo, Uniview, Axis and others by IP and login. A brand-new Hikvision is even activated for you.

🔒

No cloud — your data stays yours

Video is a short rolling buffer in RAM on your node; the long-term copy is your own Telegram chat. No account, no telemetry, no subscription.

🩺

Explains its decisions

A diagnostic journal records every rejected candidate and why, and a daily summary flags possible misses — so “why didn't it tell me?” has an answer.

How a detection happens

Each step is cheap until the next one is needed, so a small machine handles several cameras.

  1. 1 · BufferRecording from each camera into a rolling RAM buffer — no disk wear.
  2. 2 · Motion gateOnly frames that changed above the scene's noise go further.
  3. 3 · Person detectorYOLO scores people; a person must hold for consecutive frames.
  4. 4 · ChecksStill-object filter; the camera's own person signal confirms or vetoes.
  5. 5 · TelegramSnapshot with a box where events go; clip on a tap.

Install in 1 command

You need a Linux machine (amd64 or arm64, 2 GB RAM or more) that can reach api.telegram.org, IP cameras on the same network and a bot token: open @BotFather, send /newbot, copy the token.

curl -fsSL https://egorchenkov.github.io/dozorcam/install.sh | sh

Docker included

No Docker or compose plugin — the installer offers the official get.docker.com script (y/N).

Checked files

The release files go to ~/dozorcam and are checked against the release SHA256SUMS; the image is pinned by digest.

Sized for your box

The token is checked with Telegram, language and time zone come from the host, the RAM buffer from the machine memory.

It ends with a link t.me/<your bot>?start=<code> and a QR code. Prefer to do it by hand? Manual install with docker compose · non-interactive install.

Where events go

Three delivery modes. The owner switches them with /mode; cameras, their history and topics stay.

📱

One chat, no topics

Everything in one group — or, without a group, in a private chat of each person you /invite, with their own map and their own notifications. Good for 1–3 cameras.

🎥

Topic per camera

A forum group with Topics: each camera has its own topic with a pinned card, the map lives in “Control”. Good for 4 cameras and more.

🏷

Topic per location

Topics like “Home” and “Cottage”: events of several cameras inside, each with hashtags. Set a camera's place with 📍 Location on its card.

Update, backup, uninstall

The installer leaves a small helper next to the files: ~/dozorcam/dozorcam.

Update

dozorcam update

Shows what changed, pulls the release by digest, checks health and rolls back by itself if the new version does not come up. The bot tells you when there is one.

Backup & restore

dozorcam backup
dozorcam restore <file>

One file with settings, the camera list and the bot state — back on this or another machine.

Uninstall

dozorcam uninstall

Stops and removes the containers; the state and the image only if you say so. Also status, logs, restart, code.

Will it run on my box?

Count your cameras — the calculator uses numbers measured on a live installation and on test machines with fixed CPU and memory limits.

RAM4 GB
CPU4 cores
Disk2 GBthe image ~0.8 GB plus updates; video stays in RAM

Measured, not guessed

A live installation with 4 cameras on an ARM server (Neoverse-N1): about 0.5 core on average, the engine 1.6 GB with a 2 GB RAM buffer, the bot 66 MB, the image 800 MB. Plus a bench with 1–8 synthetic cameras under fixed CPU and memory limits on arm64 and amd64 — method and numbers.

What will not run

Boards with 1 GB RAM (Raspberry Pi 3, Zero 2): the system, Docker, the detector and even one camera's buffer do not fit. 32-bit ARM (armv7, older NAS) — there is no image. Pi 4 numbers are an estimate (about twice slower per core than the measured ARM).

Supported cameras

KindHow it is connected
HikvisionFound by the search; built-in RTSP and snapshot templates where ONVIF is silent. A new camera is activated from /add (password set, ONVIF user created). The camera's own person detection can gate or confirm Dozorcam's detector.
ONVIF (Profile S)Found automatically (local /24 networks and WS-Discovery); stream and snapshot addresses come from ONVIF.
By IP and login/add <IP>: typical RTSP paths of Reolink, TP-Link Tapo/VIGI, Uniview, Axis, Xiongmai/XMEye and generic ones are tried in turn; the bot shows which one worked.
Any RTSP camera/add rtsp://host:port/path, optionally a second low-resolution stream for the detector; the snapshot is taken from the stream.

Tested live on Hikvision G0/G2/G5 and Tantos cameras; other brands are checked by tests — paths and checked cameras.

Honest limits

  • No on-disk archive: the buffer holds minutes in RAM; the Telegram chat is the long-term copy.
  • Timestamps follow CCTV_TZ (the installer takes it from the host); without it they are UTC and the map warns about it.
  • The node must reach api.telegram.org; once a day it asks GitHub for the latest release (nothing is sent, CCTV_UPDATE_CHECK=0 turns it off).
  • Interface languages: English and Russian.
